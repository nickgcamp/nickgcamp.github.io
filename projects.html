Revenue forecast planner

A one-page planning model for a fictional manufacturer. You set volume, growth, new accounts, a price change and printer capacity, and it builds a 12-month forecast in three scenarios.

  • New accounts ramp up over three months instead of arriving at full volume.
  • Capacity utilization is checked against an 85% planning threshold, and the model tells you the month you'd need another printer.
  • Every number updates as you type. The math lives in one small, readable function.

Quality dashboard prototype

A remake-rate dashboard of the kind a manufacturing lab uses to spot quality problems early. It runs on 18 months of generated sample data across four product lines.

  • Filter by product and period. Every tile shows the change against the prior period.
  • A Pareto chart ranks remake reasons and shows how few of them drive most of the volume.
  • The trend chart marks the month a new design check went live, so you can see whether it worked.

Job alert tool

Job aggregators re-list postings hours or days after a company publishes them. This tool goes to the source: it reads company job boards directly every hour, so I hear about a matching role soon after it goes live.

  • Fetchers for Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby and Workday boards.
  • A seen-jobs store (SQLite or Postgres) so only new postings alert. The first run seeds quietly instead of sending hundreds of old jobs.
  • Filters for title, location and freshness, an optional 0 to 100 fit score from Claude, and email or phone alerts, best match first.
  • Runs hourly on GitHub Actions, with a test suite that needs no network.

Data graphic

The worst Vikings QBR games since 2010

After a rough Sunday in November 2026 I wanted to know where it ranked. I pulled ESPN Total QBR from game logs, kept regular-season games with at least 20 plays, ranked them with ties sharing a rank, and designed the chart so the newest game stands out.

It's the start of a side project: sports graphics built from data, one team first.
